# Chapter Twenty-Three

---

The Bloemgracht at two in the morning is one of the quietest places in the city.

The canal holds its water without moving. An occasional ring spreads from somewhere — a fish, or the memory of a boat — and then the surface is flat again. The houseboats sit without rocking. The bicycles locked along the bridge railing stand in a row, patient. A cat crosses the stone path on the far bank and disappears between two doors.

In the flat above the canal, in the back room where the window faces the water, two people are asleep.

The evening that preceded this was a good one: wine, a meal cooked at home, conversation that went where it wanted without effort. They fell asleep easily, close, the city entirely still around them. The only sound is the canal, which makes almost no sound, and now and then a distant bicycle on a larger street several blocks away.

---

At 02:14, a faint light begins.

It is located along the left side of her collarbone, beneath the skin — not on the surface, not visible except in darkness and at close range. It is the colour of something between blue and white, the colour a screen makes in an unlit room. It does not illuminate anything. It is simply present.

One long signal: three seconds, perhaps four. Then stillness for a moment.

Then several shorter ones: irregular, like a word being sent in a code whose rhythm is not quite regular — seven, eight pulses over ten seconds, spacing uneven, as if the transmission were feeling its way.

Then stillness again.

Then one final signal: three seconds, clean, and gone.

The room returns to the dark it was before.

---

Neither of them stirs. Her breathing does not change. His arm across her side is where it was. The plaster on her left index finger is where she put it before sleep.

The canal holds its water. The bicycles stand in their row. Somewhere on the far bank the cat reappears between the same two doors it disappeared between and continues on its way, unhurried.

The night continues, ordinary and complete, as if nothing has passed through it.
