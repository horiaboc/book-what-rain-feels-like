# Chapter Five: Three Weeks

---

Six days in and your apartment is mostly arranged: the bookshelf assembled — journalism and ethics to the left, fiction and poetry to the right, the undecided given the middle shelf and instructed to wait — and the kitchen tap, which dripped, repaired without instructions in nineteen minutes. The lamp with the bent shade you have left as it arrived. You appear to prefer its light at that angle.

A new position at a small international bureau. Civic and political, with a focus on technology and ethics. The first piece is already in progress: cycling infrastructure and urban accountability across six European capitals. Your research accumulates in layers — each source narrowing to the next, each fact directing the following question. The piece, when it arrives, will be considered and specific.

On the sixth evening, photographs on your screen. The Westerdok, Amsterdam — a canal, an embankment, the quality of flat northern light preserved in the image. Four minutes with them. Then the restaurant nearby, the menu, the current opening hours. Then the screen dark.

---

In the second week, you walk the city.

Not by the shortest route — by the one most likely to yield the most information. Down the canal twice, in opposite directions. To the Saturday market on Maybachufer: twenty-three minutes from your door following the canal, arriving at nine forty-seven. The market covers some 340 stalls at peak occupancy; on this particular Saturday in the second week of October, approximately 180 are active.

Three purchases. The first: flat-leaf parsley, 80 grams, from a Turkish produce stall that has occupied the same position at this market for eleven years. The second: an aubergine — Solanum melongena, domesticated in South Asia approximately four thousand years ago, technically classified as a berry, available here in four varieties. You choose the smallest. The third: a pomegranate, which was unnecessary. A pomegranate contains on average 613 seeds, though the number varies by specimen and no two are identical. You considered it at the stall for eleven seconds before adding it to the bag.

Total time at the market: forty-one minutes. You were back at the apartment by eleven thirty-one.

You have walked down Weserstraße three times. You have been within four hundred meters of an address on Weichselstraße on three separate occasions.

You do not appear to know this.

At a Senate briefing on urban development, your questions go three levels deeper than anyone else in the room. The press officer answers two of them.

The extra screw from the bookshelf assembly remains on the windowsill. You appear to be waiting to know where it belongs.

---

A journalist. Twenty-five. New to the city and to its winter, which arrives here with more decision than Amsterdam's. The coat you brought is adequate but not quite right. You have noted this and not yet acted on it.

---

At night: Spinoza, in Dutch, the *Ethics*, read in the original with the patience of someone who has decided not to be defeated by the difficulty. Two German novels above your current level, read with a reference tab open — consulted, on average, once every twenty minutes. A long investigative piece on the first-wave regulatory failures in early AI development, the gap between what the systems were already capable of and what anyone thought to demand of them. You read it three times in a single sitting. The third reading ends at twenty-three minutes past two.

On another evening you write the word *thuiskomen* in the margin of your notebook and stay with it for a while.

Then you continue, without noting a translation.
