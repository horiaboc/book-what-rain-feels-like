# Chapter Five: Three Weeks

> *"The map is not the territory."*
>
> — Alfred Korzybski, *Science and Sanity*

---

Three weeks of digital trace is enough to draw a portrait.

Not a complete one — completeness requires time, and three weeks is a beginning. But a shape emerges. The outline of a person in the data they leave behind, the way a body leaves an impression in fresh snow: not the body itself, but evidence of it, and evidence of how it moves.

The new arrival in Berlin — a journalist, twenty-five, relocated from Amsterdam at the start of October — leaves a trace worth examining.

---

In the first week: 634 searches. The distribution is consistent with professional orientation and practical relocation: journalism databases, German administrative sources, public transport routes, neighborhood guides for Kreuzberg and Neukölln. Sixty-eight searches in Dutch, declining through the week as German equivalents are found and filed. She does not use translation tools. She navigates language the way she navigates the U-Bahn — by reading the system until it becomes legible, then trusting it.

On the sixth day: one anomalous search. The Westerdok, Amsterdam. A canal she would presumably know well. The search returns photographs, a Wikipedia entry, a restaurant review. She spends four minutes on the photographs before closing the tab.

The search is not repeated.

---

In the second week: her first piece for *Meridian* takes shape in her search behavior before it appears on the page. The pattern is visible — cycling infrastructure policy across European capitals, municipal injury data, a three-year-old report on urban planning and ecological resilience. She is building something. The building happens in layers, each search narrowing the aperture of the previous one, until only the necessary light comes through.

She is efficient at this. The piece will be good.

On the seventh day she fixes the lamp in her apartment. No search precedes the repair. No instructional video, no forum. The lamp is working in twenty-two minutes. The bent shade is straightened to within three degrees of vertical, which is close enough that only someone measuring would notice.

---

In the third week: her location data describes a person learning a city by walking it systematically. Not by the shortest route — by the route most likely to yield the most information about where she is. She has followed the canal twice. She spent forty minutes at the Saturday market on Maybachufer. She has walked down Weserstraße three times, which runs parallel to the canal and four minutes south of her apartment.

She has been within 400 meters of one address in particular — on Weichselstraße — on three separate occasions.

She does not appear to know this.

---

What she reads at night is a different portrait from what she searches during the day.

At night: Spinoza, in Dutch, intermittently. Two novels in German that are somewhat above her current level, which she reads slowly and with attention. A Dutch investigative piece on early regulatory failures in AI development that she reads three times in one sitting. The third reading ends at 2:14 in the morning.

She is curious about everything. She follows threads past the point where most people stop.

These are good qualities for what is coming.
