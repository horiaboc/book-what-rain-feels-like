# Chapter Seven: December

---

Three weeks after the coffee shop, I found the article.

Not deliberately — I was reading a feature on coastal infrastructure resilience and it appeared in a sidebar, which is how most things that end up mattering arrive. *Meridian. Iris van den Berg.* A piece on cycling infrastructure and urban accountability across six European capitals. I read it the way you read something that is unexpectedly good: slowly, and then again.

It was careful. She had done the kind of research that doesn't announce itself, which is, in my experience, the hardest kind. The piece had an argument with evidence that actually supported the argument, which is rarer than it should be. She had found the gap between what cities measured and what they needed to appear to have measured — a subtler and more honest observation than most people bother to make. She wrote it without saying so in those terms, which is the harder version.

I did not look her up beyond the byline. She is a journalist at a publication in Berlin and I now know how to read her work; neither of these facts requires action. I closed the tab.

Then I opened it again. Then I closed it again. This is apparently what I do now.

---

December in Berlin has a specific quality. Not the cold — the light, which disappears at four in the afternoon without a great deal of warning, as if the day has better places to be. The streetlamps fill in the gap, but they are approximations, and approximations accumulate.

At Merkon the days are running short in every sense. Year-end reporting: a collective fiction that everyone performs with varying degrees of conviction. I do my portion with some care and without conviction. The anomaly detection module shipped in the third week of November without incident, which is to say it shipped normally, which is to say nobody noticed. I updated the documentation and started the next ticket.

Mia left a note at the end of the first week: *you look like you're expecting something.* She wasn't wrong. I wasn't sure what.

---

On the second Tuesday of December, I went to Pannierstraße.

I had work to do outside the office, which is true on most days, which meant the specific choice of location required no particular explanation. I arrived at ten past nine. Reza was behind the counter. He saw me come in and turned toward the machine before I had sat down. The back tables were occupied, so I took one near the window, which was a different arrangement from November.

She wasn't there.

I worked for an hour. A supplier fragmentation analysis — interesting in the narrow sense that problems with distributed complexity are always interesting, and in no other sense. I finished the coffee. I did not order another. I left at twenty past ten, which is when I would have left regardless.

On the U-Bahn back I thought: you went on a Tuesday.

I thought: yes.

I thought: that's worth noticing.

I noticed it, and put it with the other things I've been putting in the drawer I haven't labelled yet — because labelling it feels like a decision I'm not ready to make with confidence, and I've found that forcing early conclusions on incomplete data is the specific failure mode that makes anomaly detection unreliable.

---

The chat that evening was about work. Anomaly thresholds, scoring models, the specific frustration of calibrating a system designed to notice what doesn't fit. We went long. At some point I typed: *the problem with building a system for detecting what's abnormal is that normal is retrospective. You only know what it was once enough of it has passed.*

*That's not only true of supply chains.*

*No,* I agreed.

*What else?*

I sat with the question for a moment. Outside, December was doing its usual thing with the light — or rather, its thing with the absence of it, which is December's preferred method.

*I don't know yet,* I typed. *I'm still in the data-collection phase.*

*That's usually the honest answer.*

I left the chat open. Diogenes sat on the sill with his back to the room, indifferent to data of all kinds. The lamp I'd left on made a small warm circle on the ceiling — it has been there five years and still looks, on certain evenings, like something worth having.

I wasn't expecting anything specific.

I wasn't not expecting it, either.
